\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Accuracy}
\label{sec:res-acc}

\textbf{Fine-tuning worked.} Word recall on the six train room recordings, none of them in the fine-tuning data, rose from 63.8\% to 72.3\% (paired gain 8.5 points, 95\% interval 5.0 to 10.8; Figure~\ref{fig:accuracy}c). Nixon's own words rose from 49.3\% to 64.9\%, short replies from 32.2\% to 46.7\%, and WER fell from 39.0\% to 31.3\%.

\textbf{The corpus beat Whisper on held-out data.} On the 165 FRUS conversations the corrected layer recovered 76.1\% of reference words; Whisper alone recovered 69.2\% (Table~\ref{tab:main}, Figure~\ref{fig:accuracy}). The 6.9-point gain (6.3 to 7.7) split into 4.5 points from fine-tuned Parakeet and 2.4 from correction. The corpus won on 160 of the 165 conversations. The recognizer gap was largest on Nixon's own words and on short replies (Parakeet v5 71.6\% and 50.8\%, Whisper 63.6\% and 36.0\%).

\textbf{Correction supplied the names.} Parakeet v5 and Whisper recovered 60.2\% and 60.0\% of names. Correction lifted name recall by 19.8 points to 80.1\%, with no loss of precision: 90.3\% of displayed reference names were right, against 89.0\% for Whisper alone. By location the corrected layer recovered 85.6\% of words at Camp David, 83.7\% on the telephone, 76.6\% in the Oval Office, 76.5\% in the Cabinet Room and 49.3\% in the EOB (Figure~\ref{fig:accuracy}d).

\subsection{Artifacts of single-model transcription}
\label{sec:res-artifacts}

\textbf{Whisper alone looped, dropped speech and wrote over silence} (Figure~\ref{fig:artifacts}). Its output held 2,613 repetition loops in 1,463 conversations (55,140 words); Parakeet v5 had 105, the released corrected layer 265. Run in batches with WhisperX \citep{bain2023whisperx}, the configuration of earlier machine transcriptions of the tapes \citep{fine2026easynixon}, it discarded speech: wherever WhisperX wrote nothing for 20 seconds or more, Parakeet transcribed 5.2\% of its words, in 2,869 stretches at 60 or more words a minute. Across NARA's muted withdrawals, Whisper wrote 1,882 words in 422 conversations, typically \enquote{you} every 30 seconds, and across the corpus 987 stock phrases such as \enquote{thank you for watching}; Parakeet wrote 131 words over the same silence. No word inside a muted passage was kept in the release. Each recognizer also heard words the other missed, which is why both were kept in the corpus.

\subsection{What the second reading and the correction add}
\label{sec:res-correct}

At least one recognizer was right on 79.5\% of test reference words, 6.0 points above Parakeet alone on the same words; correction recovered 2.4 of those points. \textbf{The second reading drove the gain.} On the train room recordings, correction gained 405 reference words from Parakeet's text alone, 564 with Whisper's readings and 712 with low-confidence words also marked; before the corpus run, 340 edits helped and 20 hurt. \textbf{Same-day context corrected across recordings.} On April 9, 1971, Parakeet's \machine{want Dr. Burns, uh, Marjorie Burns? I'm pleased to call} (\conv{476-021}) became \machine{Find Dr. Burns, Arthur Burns, I placed a call}, the words of the telephone recording of the same moment (\conv{001-061}); the author confirmed both by ear. \textbf{Edits cited their evidence}: corrected test-set names cited Whisper's reading (42.8\%) or the subject log (34.8\%), and the model's own knowledge for only 4.6\%.

\subsection{Speakers}
\label{sec:res-speakers}

On the 130 test conversations outside its training data (220,664 aligned words), the classifier named 68.4\% of words, and \textbf{92.4\% of named words carried the speaker FRUS gives}; for the fourteen voices it was trained on, 95.9\% (Nixon 94.9\% over 78,305 named words, Kissinger 97.5\% over 50,743; Figure~\ref{fig:corpus}c). Cross-validated balanced accuracy was 87.2\%.
