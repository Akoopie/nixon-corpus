\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Richard Nixon's secret White House taping system recorded 2,719 hours of conversation between February 16, 1971 and July 18, 1973. Hand transcription reached only the conversations needed for trials, diplomatic histories and edited selections. All 19,426 conversations released as individual recordings were transcribed into the Nixon Corpus: 2,206 hours, 19.0 million words, every word timed and every speech segment labeled. The first publicly documented speech recognizer fine-tuned on the tapes, a Transformer model (NVIDIA's Parakeet TDT 0.6B), was paired with a second, independent recognizer (Whisper large-v3), and a large language model was used to reconcile the two readings against the National Archives' subject logs, H.\,R. Haldeman's diary and the day's other conversations. Every change was recorded as a reversible edit. Fourteen principal speakers were named by a voiceprint classifier. On 165 held-out conversations transcribed by the State Department's historians, the corpus recovered 76.1\% of words and 80.1\% of names, against 69.2\% and 60.0\% for Whisper alone. On the 130 of them outside the classifier's training data, 92.4\% of named words were attributed to the right speaker. Transcripts, model and code were released under open licenses.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} Nixon White House tapes; presidential recordings; archives; speech recognition; speaker identification; large language models; Watergate
