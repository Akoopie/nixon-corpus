\section{Data and methods}
\label{sec:methods}

Each conversation was transcribed by two recognizers: Parakeet, fine-tuned on the tapes, and Whisper, unchanged. The two outputs were merged: Parakeet's words were kept and Whisper's reading was attached at every disagreement. A large language model was then used to correct the merged text against the archive, returning a list of edits. Speakers were labeled by a voiceprint classifier (Figure~\ref{fig:pipeline}). Every layer was kept in the release.

\subsection{Sources}
\label{sec:data}

\runin{Audio.} 19,426 conversations, February 16, 1971 to July 12, 1973, 2,206 hours, were obtained from NARA's catalog copies and the Nixon Library's older per-conversation files: 6,548 telephone conversations, 6,224 in the Oval Office, 4,435 in the EOB, 2,003 at Camp David and 216 in the Cabinet Room. These made up 81.1\% of the 2,719 hours in the Nixon Library's finding aid; 44 further conversations could not be fetched, and 3,253 were available only within whole-tape files. NARA's withdrawal tones (1,242 spans in 841 conversations, 18.4 hours) were muted before transcription.

\runin{Archive.} The archival sources were NARA's conversation manifest (date, time, location, participants); the subject logs, 2,383 recovered from the Library's truncated archive and matched to 94\% of conversations; and H.\,R. Haldeman's diary, 1,473 entries from January 18, 1969 to April 30, 1973 \citep{haldeman1994diaries,nixonlib2018haldemandiaries}.

\runin{Reference transcripts} were divided into two strictly separated sets. \emph{Train:} everything used to build and tune the system: 60 WSPF transcripts (393,443 words) for fine-tuning, and eleven references of eight conversations (six room recordings, two telephone calls) that were used to set the learning rate, the correction instructions and the confidence threshold. These eight were not in the fine-tuning data; five of their room recordings, the \emph{train room recordings}, were among the corpus conversations. \emph{Test:} every FRUS document whose editors transcribed one conversation from the tape as speaker-labeled dialogue, each excerpt aligned on its own; after exclusions, 165 conversations, February 26, 1971 to June 18, 1973, 351,883 reference words (119 Oval Office, 26 telephone, 10 EOB, 5 Cabinet Room, 5 Camp David). No FRUS text reached the correction model. \emph{Registry:} NARA's WSPF and NARA transcript lists, the train references and these FRUS documents, 426 conversations. For the passages of Section~\ref{sec:cases}, both Brinkley and Nichter volumes and \citet{dean2014defense} were also searched; \citet{kutler1997abuse} and the Presidential Recordings Digital Edition were not.

\subsection{Two recognizers}
\label{sec:asr}

\runin{Parakeet, fine-tuned.} Parakeet TDT 0.6B v3, a 600-million-parameter FastConformer (a Transformer with convolution) with a token-and-duration transducer decoder \citep{xu2023tdt,nvidia2025parakeetv3}, was chosen because it was not conditioned on its own output; on the corpus it ran at least 4.3 times faster than Whisper. It was fine-tuned on 4,790 WSPF clips (19.9 hours, 49 conversations) force-aligned with wav2vec~2.0 \citep{baevski2020wav2vec2}, plus labels the author made by ear in twelve hours: 68 transcribed clips, 98 corrected chunks of low-confidence room audio, 38 noise clips on which the model had to write nothing, and 22 stretches marked with the speaker's name, each repeated ten times. Room recall rose from 63.8\% to 68.5\% (WSPF clips), 70.0\% (plus transcribed clips), 70.7\% (plus corrected chunks) and 72.3\% for the released model, v5 (plus noise clips; learning rate $6\times10^{-5}$, AdamW, 8 epochs, NVIDIA NeMo 3.0.0, \citealp{kuchaiev2019nemo}). Decoding the corpus cost US\$8.60 of rented GPU time; 88.4\% of Parakeet's 18,274,746 words received a confidence score.

\runin{Whisper and the merge.} Whisper large-v3, unchanged, transcribed 19,421 conversations, a first fifth (3,891) in batches with WhisperX (settings in the repository). The two outputs were aligned word by word. Agreed and Parakeet-only words were kept; Whisper's reading was attached at every disagreement; Whisper's words filled Parakeet's silences (no Parakeet word within 0.5 seconds, mean Whisper probability at least 0.5); Parakeet words below confidence 0.01 were marked.

\subsection{Correction against the archive}
\label{sec:correct}

Recognizers often turned names into ordinary words: Parakeet wrote \machine{the whole different} for Haldeman and \machine{Generally} for John Ehrlichman where Whisper heard the names. The correct name was often in the archival record. Google's Gemini 4 Argon \citep{google2026argon} (batch interface, temperature 0.2, October 20 to 23, 2026) was used to reconcile the two recognizers' readings with NARA's record and subject log and Haldeman's diary for that day and the next, in order to create a single corrected transcript per conversation. The merged transcript was sent in 60-second blocks, with Whisper's alternatives inline and low-confidence words marked. Short conversations of the same day were sent in one request, so that one recording could correct another. The model was instructed to keep fillers, repetitions and grammar, never to paraphrase, and to mark nonsense as \texttt{[unclear]} (instructions: \texttt{src/argon.py}). It returned edits rather than text: block, words replaced, replacement, confidence and the main evidence (\texttt{whisper}, \texttt{nara}, \texttt{diary}, \texttt{day}, \texttt{context}, \texttt{knowledge} or \texttt{sound}). 98.5\% of edits were located and applied by exact match; every edit was marked and kept reversible. All conversations with words were corrected except six. The pass used 57.3 million input and 19.4 million output tokens and cost US\$154.13 at the batch price (US\$0.07 per audio hour), or US\$308.26 at the standard price at which Google billed the batches completed by October 21.

\subsection{Speakers}
\label{sec:speakers}

Speakers were named by a logistic-regression classifier on frozen WeSpeaker ResNet-34 embeddings \citep{wang2023wespeaker}, normalized by room type, over fourteen voices (Nixon, Kissinger, Haldeman, Ehrlichman, Colson, Dean, Ziegler, Mitchell, Rogers, Shultz, Haig, Woods, Connally, Butterfield) or \enquote{other}. It was trained on 24,656 clips from 902 conversations: WSPF speaker turns, two-person recordings on which Nixon was confidently ruled out (bringing in Kissinger, Haig, Rogers and others), and the author's 22 marks. Each Whisper segment of at least 1.5 seconds was scored among the people NARA listed as present and was named at probability 0.7 or more.

\subsection{Evaluation}
\label{sec:eval}

Hypotheses and references were normalized with Whisper's English normalizer \citep{radford2023whisper}, cut to their shared stretch and aligned word by word. The measures were word recall, $1-(S+D)/N$; word error rate, $(S+D+I)/N$; name recall and name precision over the reference's names (words capitalized mid-sentence at least twice and in at least 80\% of their uses; a wrong name absent from the reference was not counted); and recall on Nixon's turns and on replies of four words or fewer. Test intervals were 95\% bootstrap intervals over conversations, paired between layers. The test set informed no decision and shared no conversation with the train set. Of 231 FRUS conversations, 66 were excluded: 44 not in the corpus (43 released only within whole-tape files, one released after the conversation list was built), 9 the author had heard, labeled or corrected, 7 parallel recordings of excluded conversations and 6 whose audio held a different conversation. Because FRUS text could have been in the correction model's pretraining data, the merged layer, untouched by any language model, was scored beside the corrected layer.
