\section{Data and methods}
\label{sec:methods}

Each conversation was transcribed by two speech recognizers: Parakeet, fine-tuned on the tapes (further trained on examples from them), and Whisper, used unchanged. The two transcripts were merged by keeping Parakeet's words and attaching Whisper's reading wherever the two disagreed. A large language model was then used to correct the merged text against the archive, returning a list of edits. Speakers were labeled by a voiceprint classifier (Figure~\ref{fig:pipeline}). Every layer, meaning each stage's version of the transcript, was kept in the release.

\subsection{Sources}
\label{sec:data}

\runin{Audio.} Recordings of 19,426 conversations, 2,206 hours from February 16, 1971 to July 12, 1973, were obtained from NARA's catalog copies and the Nixon Library's older per-conversation files. They comprised 6,548 telephone conversations, 6,224 in the Oval Office, 4,435 in the EOB, 2,003 at Camp David and 216 in the Cabinet Room, and made up 81.1\% of the 2,719 hours in the Nixon Library's finding aid. A further 44 conversations could not be downloaded, and 3,261 were available only within whole-tape files; eight of the 19,426 recordings were Library files that NARA's catalog did not list. NARA's withdrawal tones, which mark passages withheld from release (1,242 spans in 841 conversations, 18.4 hours), were muted before transcription.

\runin{Archive.} NARA's conversation manifest gave the date, time, location and participants of each conversation. The subject logs, 2,383 of which were recovered from the Library's truncated archive, were matched to 94\% of conversations. The diary of Chief of Staff H.\,R. Haldeman supplied 1,473 entries from January 18, 1969 to April 30, 1973 \citep{haldeman1994diaries,nixonlib2018haldemandiaries}.

\runin{Reference transcripts.} These human transcripts, against which the machine output was measured, were divided into two strictly separated sets, Train and Test. The \emph{Train} set held everything used to build and tune the system: 60 WSPF transcripts (393,443 words) used for fine-tuning, and eleven reference transcripts of eight conversations (six room recordings and two telephone calls) used to set the learning rate, the correction instructions and the confidence threshold. These eight conversations were not in the fine-tuning data. Their six room recordings made up the \emph{train room recordings}; five of them were corpus conversations and were used to score the corpus layers. The \emph{Test} set comprised every FRUS document whose editors had transcribed a single conversation from the tape as speaker-labeled dialogue, each excerpt aligned separately. After exclusions it held 165 conversations from February 26, 1971 to June 18, 1973, with 351,883 reference words (119 Oval Office, 26 telephone, 10 EOB, 5 Cabinet Room and 5 Camp David). No FRUS text reached the correction model. The \emph{registry} of published transcripts combined NARA's lists of WSPF and NARA transcripts, the Train references and these FRUS documents, and covered 426 conversations. For the passages of Section~\ref{sec:cases}, both volumes by Brinkley and Nichter and \citet{dean2014defense} were also searched; \citet{kutler1997abuse} and the Presidential Recordings Digital Edition were not.

\subsection{Two recognizers}
\label{sec:asr}

\runin{Parakeet, fine-tuned.} Parakeet TDT 0.6B v3, a 600-million-parameter FastConformer with a token-and-duration transducer decoder \citep{xu2023tdt,nvidia2025parakeetv3}, was chosen because it was not conditioned on its own output, and on the corpus it ran at least 4.3 times faster than Whisper. To adapt it to the tapes, it was fine-tuned on 4,790 WSPF clips (19.9 hours from 49 conversations), whose transcripts were aligned to the audio with wav2vec~2.0 \citep{baevski2020wav2vec2}, plus labels from the author's twelve hours of listening, each repeated ten times: 68 transcribed clips, 98 corrected chunks of low-confidence room audio and 38 noise clips on which the model had to learn to write nothing. Each addition raised word recall, the share of reference words transcribed correctly, on the room recordings of the Train set: from 63.8\% to 68.5\% with the WSPF clips, 70.0\% with the transcribed clips, 70.7\% with the corrected chunks and 72.3\% with the noise clips. This last model, v5, was released (learning rate $6\times10^{-5}$, AdamW, 8 epochs, NVIDIA NeMo 3.0.0, \citealp{kuchaiev2019nemo}). Transcribing the corpus cost US\$8.60 of rented GPU time, and 88.4\% of Parakeet's 18,274,746 words received a confidence score.

\runin{Whisper and the merge.} Whisper large-v3, used unchanged, transcribed 19,421 conversations; the first fifth of them (3,891) were run in batches with WhisperX (settings in the repository). The two transcripts were then aligned word by word. Words that both recognizers wrote, or only Parakeet wrote, were kept, and Whisper's reading was attached at every disagreement. Where Parakeet was silent (no Parakeet word within 0.5 seconds), Whisper's words filled the gap if their mean probability was at least 0.5. Parakeet words with confidence below 0.01 were marked.

\subsection{Correction against the archive}
\label{sec:correct}

Recognizers often turned names into ordinary words. Parakeet, for example, wrote \machine{the whole different} for Haldeman and \machine{Generally} for John Ehrlichman, Nixon's domestic policy adviser, where Whisper heard the names. The correct name was often in the archival record. Google's Gemini 4 Argon \citep{google2026argon}, a large language model, was used to reconcile the two recognizers' readings with NARA's record and subject log and with Haldeman's diary for that day and the next, producing a single corrected transcript for each conversation. It was run through the batch interface at temperature 0.2 from October 20 to 23, 2026. The merged transcript was sent in 60-second blocks, with Whisper's alternative readings inline and low-confidence words marked. Short conversations from the same day were sent together in one request, so that one recording could correct another. The model was instructed to keep fillers, repetitions and grammar, never to paraphrase, and to mark nonsense as \texttt{[unclear]} (see \texttt{src/argon.py}). Instead of rewritten text, it returned a list of edits, each giving the block, the words replaced, the replacement, a confidence and the main evidence: \texttt{whisper}, \texttt{nara}, \texttt{diary}, \texttt{day}, \texttt{context}, \texttt{knowledge} or \texttt{sound}. Of these edits, 98.5\% were located and applied by exact match, and every edit was marked and kept reversible. Every conversation containing words was corrected except six. The pass used 57.3 million input and 19.4 million output tokens. It cost US\$154.13 at the batch price (US\$0.07 per audio hour), or US\$308.26 at the standard price, at which Google billed the batches completed by October 21.

\subsection{Speakers}
\label{sec:speakers}

Each speech segment was represented by a voiceprint, an embedding from the frozen (not retrained) WeSpeaker ResNet-34 model \citep{wang2023wespeaker}, normalized by room type. A logistic-regression classifier assigned each voiceprint to one of fourteen voices (Nixon, Kissinger, Haldeman, Ehrlichman, Colson, Dean, Ziegler, Mitchell, Rogers, Shultz, Haig, Woods, Connally, Butterfield) or to \enquote{other}. It was trained on 24,656 clips from 902 conversations: WSPF speaker turns, two-person recordings on which Nixon was confidently ruled out (bringing in Kissinger, Haig, Rogers and others), and the author's 22 marks. Each Whisper segment of at least 1.5 seconds was scored only against the people NARA listed as present and was named when the classifier gave a person a probability of 0.7 or more.

\subsection{Evaluation}
\label{sec:eval}

Each machine transcript and its reference were normalized with Whisper's English normalizer \citep{radford2023whisper}, cut to the stretch they shared and aligned word by word. Word recall, $1-(S+D)/N$, measured the share of the $N$ reference words that were neither substituted ($S$) nor deleted ($D$); the word error rate (WER), $(S+D+I)/N$, also counted inserted words ($I$). Name recall and name precision were computed over the names in the reference, taken to be words capitalized mid-sentence at least twice and in at least 80\% of their uses; a wrong name absent from the reference was not counted. Recall was also measured on Nixon's turns and on replies of four words or fewer. On the Test set, 95\% intervals were obtained by bootstrap resampling of conversations, paired between layers. The Test set informed no decision and shared no conversation with the Train set. Of 231 FRUS conversations, 66 were excluded: 44 were not in the corpus (43 released only within whole-tape files, one released after the conversation list was built), 9 had been heard, labeled or corrected by the author, 7 were parallel recordings of excluded conversations, and 6 had audio holding a different conversation. Because FRUS text could have been in the correction model's training data, the merged layer, which no language model touched, was scored alongside the corrected layer.
